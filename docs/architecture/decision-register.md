<a id="access-aware-start"></a>
## Access-aware default launch — DECIDED, details scoped separately

**Decision date/source:** 21 October 2026, explicit repository-owner instruction in the architecture discussion.

- A person with [full rights over the particular quiz](#full-rights-practice-authority) starts a new attempt in training format by default: no timer and immediate feedback after each accepted answer. D03/D12 still define the feedback boundary: acceptance locks the answer before correctness, the correct answer and explanation are released. This does not introduce answer-key disclosure before acceptance.
- A person whose rights permit only taking the quiz follows the policy or policies provided by its creator. Timing, disclosure and other execution constraints come from the applicable authorized creator policy. The participant cannot turn that start into unrestricted practice by supplying a different mode or missing duration.
- For an ordinary launch, the participant chooses among the policies authorized for that participant and quiz. If several are available and the choice is ambiguous, [request selection before creating an attempt](#ambiguous-ordinary-policy-selection). The full-rights training default remains available in this ordinary context. For a launch within an assignment, the attempt strictly follows that assignment's policy; an alternative ordinary-launch policy cannot replace it, including when the participant has full rights over the quiz. A separate practice attempt does not count as completing the assignment.
- Resolve the authenticated subject, resource-specific authority and authorized launch context server-side, then persist the selected policy/version and effective settings with the new attempt. A client-supplied role, owner or mode does not establish authority.
- An already-started attempt retains its timing and disclosure policy. Its timer cannot be switched off by editing the quiz or obtaining different rights. Pause is available only under its saved approved rules; an authorized untimed run is a separate new attempt. Existing explicit timing-adjustment and cancellation rules remain scoped to their approved operations.
- If a full-rights user explicitly enters a policy-bound self-test, that attempt obeys the selected policy; the default practice path does not rewrite its timer or disclosure later.

**Example:** a creator permits both untimed practice and a 15-minute assessment for ordinary launches. A learner chooses either authorized policy there. An assignment bound to the 15-minute assessment always starts with that policy and its feedback restrictions; choosing practice elsewhere does not complete the assignment. The creator's ordinary default training run is still untimed with immediate post-answer feedback, but their own participation in the assignment follows its assessment policy. Later policy edits cannot remove an active attempt's captured timer.

<a id="full-rights-practice-authority"></a>
**Full-rights refinement — DECIDED, 22 October 2026:** the repository owner selected option A: free practice is available to the quiz owner and to users with full rights to edit the questions and correct answers of that particular quiz.

- Resolve ownership or full content-editing authority for the authenticated subject and the specific quiz on the server. Full content-editing authority covers the quiz's questions and correct answers, rather than only a subset of its items or metadata. A qualifying editor does not need an additional separate free-practice grant.
- Taking/viewing the quiz, editing its title/tags, editing an isolated question, or holding rights over a different quiz does not establish this authority. A teacher, moderator or administrator role name alone is insufficient: delegated and administrative access qualifies when its effective permissions actually confer full content editing for this quiz.
- A take-only participant can still use a practice policy explicitly authorized by the creator. The owner/full-editor criterion establishes entitlement to the ordinary free-practice default; it does not remove authorized practice policies from other participants.
- Apply existing resource, organization/tenant and membership boundaries as relevant; missing or ambiguous identity/authority cannot establish free-practice eligibility. This decision grants no new role permissions and does not select a new delegation, ownership-transfer or security-bypass mechanism. Mapping the approved criterion to existing permission and resource checks is engineering preparation.
- This authority supplies the ordinary untimed training default with feedback after immutable answer acceptance. Assignment participation remains strictly bound to its assignment policy, including for owners and full editors. Gaining editing rights cannot switch off the timer or change disclosure within an already captured attempt.

**Example:** Anna owns a quiz and Boris is authorized to edit all its questions and correct answers. Both can start ordinary free practice without a separate practice grant. Vera may only rename the quiz, and Gleb may only take it; neither receives unrestricted practice from those rights. Boris launching an assignment still follows its timed assessment policy.

<a id="ambiguous-ordinary-policy-selection"></a>
**Ambiguous ordinary-launch refinement — DECIDED, 22 October 2026:** the repository owner selected option A: if several policies are authorized for an ordinary launch and the choice is ambiguous, request an explicit choice before creating the attempt.

- Resolve the authenticated participant, quiz, launch context and authorized policies before start. When no explicit choice, established default or compatible legacy mapping determines a single applicable policy, ask the participant to choose among their authorized alternatives. Do not select the first, least restrictive or an implicitly invented creator-default policy to conceal ambiguity.
- While that choice is pending, create no attempt, placeholder attempt, form or attempt-bound policy snapshot; start no timer and consume no allowed attempt. Do not emit `AttemptStarted` evidence. The selection response contains only authorized, participant-safe policy information needed to understand the alternatives, without question content or answer keys.
- After selection, revalidate the chosen policy and resource authority through the normal start boundary and persist the resolved immutable policy with the newly created attempt. Merely displaying an option does not grant authority to launch it later; current start eligibility still applies. Exact request/response, version-conflict and error representation remains engineering preparation.
- Preserve the already approved owner/full-editor practice default and strict assignment policy binding. Those established contexts do not become ambiguous merely because other ordinary policies exist. Unambiguous authorized legacy mappings remain compatible; an ambiguous legacy request must obtain a choice before start, with the required client support included in activation readiness.
- This refinement decides multiple-policy ambiguity. It grants no unrestricted-practice fallback when no policy is authorized and does not define a new single-policy confirmation requirement or HTTP status. [Legacy `TIMED` with a disabled quiz timer](#legacy-timed-with-disabled-timer) is decided separately below.

**Example:** a take-only participant may choose untimed practice or a twenty-minute assessment. A generic ordinary start cannot distinguish them, so the system requests a choice and creates no attempt. Selecting the assessment starts its twenty-minute timer only through a successful subsequent start; time spent choosing consumes no attempt duration or attempt allowance. A changed or revoked authorization must be revalidated before that start.

<a id="legacy-timed-with-disabled-timer"></a>
**Legacy TIMED refinement — DECIDED, 22 October 2026:** the repository owner selected option A: preserve an untimed start for a legacy `TIMED` request when the quiz timer is disabled and an authorized untimed policy is unambiguously resolved.

- Apply this rule at the legacy request compatibility boundary for a new attempt. The quiz timer must be explicitly disabled and the server must resolve one applicable authorized policy without a timer. The old enum value alone neither creates a time limit nor grants practice rights. A stored duration for a disabled quiz timer does not turn that timer on.
- Preserve the other approved legacy delivery, navigation, grading, disclosure and response contracts; this timing mapping does not authorize additional practice capabilities or rename legacy response enums. Capture the effective untimed policy/version with the new attempt and retain compatibility fixtures for the legacy representation.

- Assignment binding and required timing in the resolved policy take precedence over the disabled legacy quiz flag. An enabled/required timer with missing or invalid duration does not qualify for this compatibility rule; do not drop its timer or invent a duration. Validate the effective configuration before creation under the existing policy-validation boundary. Exact invalid-configuration response details remain preparation.
- This is a mapping for new legacy starts, not permission to disable an active attempt's saved timer or rewrite historical mode/policy evidence. A new policy-based request follows its explicit resolved configuration; unsupported combinations still require implementation and conformance before activation.

**Example:** an old client sends `TIMED` for a quiz whose timer is off, even though an inactive fifteen-minute duration remains stored. The authorized launch resolves unambiguously to an untimed compatible policy, so the attempt starts without a timer, as before. If the launch instead belongs to an assignment requiring twenty minutes, the old quiz flag cannot remove that assignment limit.

**Remaining details:** launch-context/policy references, remaining compatibility mappings and selection/unavailable/invalid-configuration error contracts remain in [R01](open-questions.md#oq-r01). Choice before an ambiguous start, assignment binding, full-rights eligibility and this disabled-timer compatibility case are decided. Exact mapping to existing permission checks remains preparation, not an unresolved product choice or permission to add role grants. **Example:** with the legacy default, question 3 must be answered before question 4. With the authorized skip/return configuration, a participant may leave question 3 unanswered, answer question 4 and later return to answer question 3. ## Navigation, answer finalization and pending confirmation — CAPABILITIES DECIDED

**Decision date/source:** 21 October 2026, explicit repository-owner instruction to support free navigation, finalized answers with or without read-only revisit, answers editable until final attempt submission, and deferred confirmation with a retained proposed answer. Existing behavior remains the default; these are authorized policy configurations, not participant-controlled changes to an active attempt.

The owner's numbered scenarios are combinations of independent dimensions, not five mutually exclusive modes. Scenario 4 duplicates scenario 2b. Free navigation alone neither grants editing nor reveals protected feedback.

| Dimension | Approved capability | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Navigation | Free switching within the authorized form, or the existing sequential behavior; configured skip/return is supported. | Form membership, delivered-content restrictions, assignment policy and section constraints still apply. |
| Finalized-item revisit | Permit read-only revisit, or prohibit revisiting a finalized item during the active attempt. | A read-only view never unlocks the answer or releases feedback earlier. A no-revisit policy prevents future server retrieval; it cannot erase content already delivered to a browser. Post-completion review remains a separate policy. |
| Answer finalization | Lock on individual answer confirmation, on section submission, or on final attempt submission. | The latter two retain provisional responses until the applicable boundary. [Section submission](#section-answer-finalization) permanently closes that section; final-attempt editing cannot reopen it. |
| Deferred confirmation | Save an optional proposed answer, mark it as requiring explicit confirmation and present it again in the final confirmation flow with the saved proposal. | The mark is not a final answer or an ordinary bookmark. While confirmation is pending, the item remains unanswered for final scoring/completion. |

**Confirmed semantics:**

- In the editable-until-final-submission configuration, an ordinary response remains revisable before that boundary and is finalized through the approved completion flow. Saving a response is distinct from final immutable acceptance. This capability does not retroactively reopen previously finalized answers or alter the legacy immediate-lock practice contract.
- A response marked as requiring confirmation must not be automatically accepted by a generic save, navigation action or final-submit operation. If the attempt completes while the mark remains, that item counts as unanswered even when a proposed answer is stored. No accepted incorrect answer is fabricated. The [saved completion requirement](#voluntary-completion-requirement), disabled by default, determines whether voluntary incomplete submission is allowed; expiry follows its separate approved outcome.
- The final confirmation flow must bring pending items back with their retained proposals; simply reaching that flow does not confirm them. Reload/reconnect preserves both the proposal and its pending-confirmation state, subject to the saved policy and lifecycle.
- A finalized answer cannot be converted back into an unanswered pending item by applying a mark. The capability concerns provisional/unanswered work and does not revoke already accepted evidence.
- Existing disclosure rules remain: correctness, the correct answer and protected explanation follow immutable acceptance. A response that remains editable must not gain those fields. The default practice path still locks and then releases immediate feedback; delaying finalization delays such feedback. This decision does not approve answer changes after protected feedback or a new retry/scoring policy.
- No-revisit and editing settings must be coherent with navigation and finalization. For example, forbidding revisit of finalized items does not by itself forbid returning to an unanswered pending item. The typed policy validator and server allowed actions must reject or disable combinations whose promised confirmation/edit path is unreachable.

**Example:** with editing until final submission, question 2 has an ordinary response that may be changed before completion. Question 3 has proposed answer B and is marked as requiring confirmation. The final confirmation flow shows question 3 with B, but an attempt completed without confirming it leaves question 3 unanswered. Question 2 is finalized through the ordinary completion flow. In an immediate-lock configuration, confirming question 2 locks it at once; a separate revisit setting determines whether it can then be viewed.

**Expiry refinement:** the owner approved [automatic finalization of ordinary saved responses at expiry](#editable-response-expiry) for this additional editable configuration. Pending-confirmation proposals remain unanswered.

**Section refinement:** [editing until section submission](#section-answer-finalization) is approved. Pending questions are presented for confirmation before their section closes; submitting it permanently ends editing in that section.

**Completion refinement:** [requiring all questions to be answered before voluntary submission](#voluntary-completion-requirement) is policy-controlled and disabled by default. It does not block or extend time expiry.

**Remaining interactions:** exact compatibility read surfaces and the transactional D07/D09 command composition are tracked in [open questions](open-questions.md#oq-answer-finalization). [Closed-section completeness](#closed-section-completeness), [section-timeout scope](#section-timeout-scope), [automatic completion at last-unclosed-section expiry](#last-unclosed-section-expiry) and [start/break clock activation with separate delivery, including prohibited breaks](#section-start-and-break) are decided. ## Expiry finalization for editable responses — DECIDED

**Decision date/source:** 21 October 2026, repository owner selected option A: when time expires, finalize ordinary saved responses and leave marked pending-confirmation items unanswered.

Otherwise the next permitted section follows the [saved break/start policy](#section-start-and-break): explicit early start or automatic clock start at a permitted break's expiry; immediate clock start at previous-section closure when breaks are prohibited. **Example:** the server has nine ordinary saved editable responses and one proposal marked as requiring confirmation when the attempt's time expires. ## Section timeout scope — DECIDED

**Decision date/source:** 21 October 2026, repository owner selected option A: by default a section timeout closes only that section and permits continuation; a policy may instead terminate the whole attempt.

- Capture the timeout outcome in the resolved policy before start. The default closes and permanently locks the expired section while allowing continuation to a later permitted section, if one exists. When that closure leaves no unclosed section, [complete the attempt automatically](#last-unclosed-section-expiry). A separately authorized whole-attempt outcome ends the attempt on any section expiry and permits no further section participation.
- In either outcome, preserve the approved response treatment: finalize eligible ordinary saved editable responses, keep finalized answers immutable, and leave pending-confirmation/empty items unanswered. An enabled require-all setting cannot block expiry or extend the deadline.
- Overall attempt expiry or assignment close always prevents continuation, including when it coincides with a section timeout. Section-only handling cannot override those saved outer deadlines or bypass other section-access restrictions.
- Reuse the shared idempotent expiry boundary. Concurrent workers, request-time checks, manual section submission and reconnect must resolve to one durable outcome, with no reopened section, duplicated acceptance or advancement after terminal expiry. Closure does not itself release feedback.
- The [approved start/break policy](#section-start-and-break) determines clock activation separately from question delivery. A permitted break allows explicit early start and has a five-minute default maximum; at break expiry the next clock starts without delivering a question. With breaks prohibited, the next permitted section's clock starts immediately at previous-section closure, still without delivering a question. After expiry closes the last unclosed section, there is no next-section break or start action.

**Example:** Grammar expires after ten minutes and Vocabulary remains. Under the default, Grammar closes and the participant may continue with Vocabulary while outer deadlines still permit it. Under the configured whole-attempt outcome, the attempt ends and Vocabulary cannot be taken. Grammar's pending proposals remain unanswered in both cases.

<a id="section-start-and-break"></a>
## Next-section clock, bounded breaks and explicit question delivery — DECIDED

**Decision date/source:** 21 October 2026, explicit repository-owner instructions: start the next section after an explicit participant action by default; policy controls break availability and maximum duration, default five minutes (`PT5M`). At break expiry, start the next section's timer automatically but wait for the participant's request before delivering its next question.

- Distinguish closure of the previous section, permission to continue, break/wait state, next-section clock activation and question delivery. Where a break is permitted, an authorized explicit start before its expiry may activate the next clock. When the break expires first, the next clock starts automatically at the saved break deadline, provided the attempt and outer deadlines still permit continuation. Where breaks are prohibited, apply the immediate-start refinement below.
- Clock activation alone never delivers a question, prefetches it into a hidden client payload or records an item as delivered. After automatic activation, the participant must explicitly request the next question. The server checks current authority, section state and deadlines before delivery; a successful delivery request neither resets nor starts a fresh duration clock.
- While no question has been requested after automatic activation, the participant view shows the running timer and an action such as "Next question", with no question content. Reload/reconnect returns that same undelivered state and actual remaining time. Once an item has actually been delivered, ordinary resume/navigation rules apply; this decision does not erase its delivery evidence.
- The saved policy determines whether a break is allowed and its maximum duration. The default maximum for a permitted inter-section break is five minutes, not an unlimited wait. No additional numerical limits are inferred.
- This break is between sections; it does not stop or disable a timer for an already active section, reopen the previous section, pause the total-attempt clock, or extend the assignment deadline. An earlier outer deadline can shorten the available waiting time and prevents later section activation.
- Persist authoritative break/start timing and separate delivery state. The automatic start is the saved break deadline for a permitted break, or the previous section's effective closure time when breaks are prohibited; it is never reconnect, worker execution or first-question request time. Repeated commands and manual-start/expiry races must converge on one start/deadline. If section or outer time has already expired before delivery is requested, apply the saved expiry policy rather than granting time or serving the expired question.
- This refines older D31/R17 wording that could be read as always starting the next section immediately when the previous one closes. Closing remains atomic and irreversible; the saved break policy determines whether clock activation follows immediately or after waiting. Question delivery remains a separate action. Conformance must cover both paths before activation.

**Example:** Grammar closes at 10:00; the permitted break ends at 10:05; Vocabulary has a 15-minute duration. Without an earlier explicit start, its clock starts at 10:05. A participant returning at 10:10 sees ten minutes remaining and a "Next question" action, without the question. Requesting it at 10:11 leaves nine minutes, not a new fifteen-minute allowance. Earlier outer expiry still wins.

<a id="no-break-section-transition"></a>
**Prohibited-break refinement — DECIDED, 22 October 2026:** the repository owner selected option A: when policy prohibits breaks, start the next section's timer immediately after the previous section closes, and deliver its question only on request.

- Apply this transition after both successful voluntary section submission and section-only expiry, provided a next authorized section exists and the attempt remains eligible to continue. A rejected submission does not close the current section or start another timer. Whole-attempt expiry, earlier/equal outer deadlines and terminal completion prevent further activation.
- The next clock starts at the previous section's authoritative effective closure time. For an expired section, use its saved deadline rather than delayed expiry processing. No intermediate waiting allowance or participant start confirmation postpones that clock; the five-minute default applies only to permitted breaks.
- Preserve explicit question delivery, the undelivered state, authorization checks and remaining-time display. Neither the closure response nor background activation may silently include the next question. A later question request does not reset the clock. If time has already elapsed, apply expiry, including automatic attempt completion when that closes the last unclosed section.
- Persist one coherent closure/start outcome and make retries, duplicate submissions and competing expiry checks idempotent. This decision changes no duration and introduces no timer into an authorized untimed policy. Current runtime defaults and already captured policy contracts retain their compatibility/activation gates.

**Example without a break:** Grammar closes at 10:00 and Vocabulary has ten minutes. Vocabulary's clock starts at 10:00. A participant requesting its first question at 10:03 has seven minutes left, with no question marked delivered before that request. If Grammar expired at 10:00 but a worker observes that at 10:03, Vocabulary still has only seven minutes left.

**Decision date/source:** 22 October 2026, the repository owner approved extending the pending email's retry deadline to seven days from a new correction, without postponing the next retry or changing the daily frequency.

- For an eligible new actual visible score correction consolidated into still-active pending email for the same recipient/result, extend the retry deadline to seven days from the correction's authoritative committed time. Preserve the next scheduled retry and the once-per-day cadence. Updating the score does not itself add an immediate extra sending attempt.
- Use immutable correction identity/version and its persisted time, not delayed event processing or worker restart time. A duplicate event, an older out-of-order event, unchanged visible score or another sending error must not extend the deadline or replace a newer pending score. Extension must not shorten an existing deadline.
- The pending delivery can consequently last more than seven days and perform more than seven daily retries across distinct actual corrections. Once new corrections stop, the remaining retries are bounded by the effective deadline; successful sending or opt-out cancellation still stops them. Do not impose the original seven-retry total as a cap that prevents the approved extension.
- Keep extension, latest-score selection and dispatch coordination consistent under concurrent workers/corrections. A newer correction must not be lost when completion of an older in-flight message is recorded. Preserve the distinction between pending, already handed-off and successfully sent work and the existing uncertain-outcome boundary.
- This rule extends active pending retry work. It does not replay previously sent messages or revive cancelled/expired work merely because an old event is processed again. [Opt-out cancels pending emails, and re-enabling does not restore them](#score-correction-email-opt-out-cancellation). [One explicit authorized manual attempt after automatic exhaustion](#single-manual-score-correction-email-attempt) is a separate action, not an automatic deadline extension. A genuinely new correction remains subject to the existing notification eligibility rules; preference/dispatch races and other terminal-state handling remain separate contracts.
- Preserve the committed result, immutable correction/audit history and one logical in-app notification per actual change. Only pending email delivery is consolidated and its deadline extended; access/release checks and email opt-in are unchanged.

**Example:** the initial temporary failure is on day zero, with a retry deadline on day seven. On day six the visible score changes from 18/20 to 16/20. **Consumers:** #675 R05 and #800, durable pending delivery and correction-event processing. ### Bounded certificate examinations and configurable completion — DECIDED; IMPLEMENTATION PREPARATION

**Owner refinement, 22 October 2026:** a certificate-bearing test always has configured finite submission/attempt bounds and a finite number of permitted attempts. The default allowance is **one attempt**. On an eligible successful outcome, the default is **immediate certificate issuance**. No numerical duration is selected by this refinement. These target defaults must be represented explicitly in the saved configuration; they do not silently replace the existing formal-release contract or legacy training behavior.

Keep three concepts separate: the participant's opportunity to complete the examination, release of their result, and release of their certificate. With one attempt, result availability may be deferred until the configured deadline or responsible-person approval. With multiple attempts, a finite submission window may run from the first attempt's start; a later attempt does not restart that same first-start window. Certificate conditions may require immediate issuance after success, approval, or reaching a separately configured certificate-release date/deadline. A later certificate-release date does not extend permission to answer. Approval is a release/issuance gate, not approval of the otherwise deferred manual-grading feature.

**Examples:** (1) one attempt, successful eligible result released immediately: issue the certificate immediately; (2) one attempt, result release after Friday's deadline or an authorized approval: show the applicable pending state and do not disclose or certify an unreleased result; (3) three attempts within seven days of the first start, with certificate release on a separately configured date: subsequent starts do not reset the seven-day window. The seven-day value is illustrative, not a default.

The earlier finality invariant remains: do not issue provisionally and later revoke merely because the participant uses ordinary continuation in the same examination. The new immediate-success option means an unused numerical allowance alone must not be interpreted as a requirement to wait until the submission deadline.

**Q42 clarification, 22 October 2026:** support the discussed completion modes and configure the intended mode when creating the certificate-bearing quiz, captured in its versioned execution/assignment policy. This is not a global choice of automatic completion for every multi-attempt examination. Support automatic finalization on an eligible success, closing remaining opportunities, and a learner-choice mode offering finalization/issuance versus continued improvement within the saved limits. In learner-choice mode, continuing does not issue a provisional certificate; the approved best-result/risk-retake rules remain. Show the configured consequence before start. The existing one-attempt/immediate-success default remains; no additional global default for ambiguous multi-attempt configurations is inferred.

**Q43/Q44 approved:** configured mandatory certificate conditions compose with AND: finality, eligible released result and each configured approval/date condition must all hold. Approval on Thursday with release not before Friday issues no earlier than Friday; approval only on Monday delays issuance until Monday. When issuance is deferred until after the submission deadline, a passing score alone does not consume or close otherwise eligible improvement opportunities: the participant can continue under the saved completion mode, allowance, window and result-selection rule. A delayed certificate is not itself a reason to stop attempts. Finality and release checks remain atomic with applicable command/version guards; approval for an old result cannot silently approve a different issuing result.

**Engineering preparation:** identify the examination/assignment scope, immutable window/allowance and release-condition configuration, persisted finality evidence, and concurrency with in-flight/new starts, extension, extra-attempt grants, result corrections, approval of a specific result version and issuance retries. Unlimited attempts or an unbounded completion window are not valid executable certificate-examination configurations. Future reopening/extra-attempt rules cannot silently invalidate an already issued valid certificate. Do not introduce a new default duration, automatic approval deadline, certificate-linked revocation option or public identity disclosure.

Existing A26 correction/revocation/supersession addresses a genuinely corrected or invalidated issuing result; it is not ordinary continuation after a successful exam. Preserve the previously approved independent certificate notification rules. [Preparation evidence](open-questions.md#preparation-evidence) distinguishes this future certificate gate from the currently implemented legacy attempt lifecycle.
